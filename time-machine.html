How much would you like to prune?

“Pruning” means selling some of what your plant has gained. Think of a gardener: prune a little and you bank some profit but keep most of the plant growing; prune a lot and you bank more, but have less left to grow if prices keep climbing. Pick a style — we’ll also compare all three at the end.

What’s pruning? (worked example)

Say the plant bought £10 of Bitcoin, and the price then rises 10%, so that bit is now worth £11. That’s when it’s time to prune:

  • Light: sells a quarter (£2.75) and keeps £8.25 growing.
  • Medium: sells half (£5.50) and keeps £5.50 growing.
  • Heavy: sells all £11 and starts fresh.

Sold money goes into your pot as cash. It’s pretend money, so there’s nothing to lose from trying all three.

About the prices

Use your own price file instead

Choose a CSV with the columns Date,BTC_GBP (dates as YYYY-MM-DD, prices in pounds). We never make up prices — rows we can't read are skipped, and journeys with big holes in the data are refused.

Please read: Time Machine is a simulation using past daily closing prices. It never touches real money, exchange accounts or API keys, and it cannot place real trades. Past results are not forecasts and nothing here is financial advice. Real life has costs this simulation leaves out — price differences between buying and selling, delays, minimum order sizes, and tax on any gains. Bitcoin prices from its earliest years are thin and less reliable. Bitcoin can lose most of its value.
