🐂 Bull markets, 🐻 bear markets & Bitcoin's four-year cycle

🐂 Bull market: a long period when Bitcoin's price generally rises. 🐻 Bear market: a long period when its price generally falls.

Why four years? In some earlier cycles Bitcoin recovered, grew for several years, then experienced a big downturn. Bitcoin's mining reward also halves approximately every four years. But this isn't a reliable schedule: prices can rise or fall at any time.

Important: Tough Times, Middle Ground and Boom Times are historical 10th, 50th and 90th percentile examples, not guaranteed outcomes or a prediction that the next bull or bear market will happen on schedule. The future could be outside this range.

Nobody can know what Bitcoin will do. So we look back at what happened after every past planting day, and show three futures from that history: a rough patch, a typical run and a good run. It’s a weather forecast, not a promise.

⚙️ How the forecast is worked out (optional)

Every past day since the start year you choose is treated as a pretend planting day. We see what the money was worth after your chosen time, then show the 10th, 50th and 90th best results out of 100.

Bitcoin’s earliest years had huge jumps that may never repeat, so older history can look rosier than it should.

About the prices

Bitcoin launched on 3 January 2009 without an established exchange price. Bitcoin trading markets began appearing in 2010, but this app currently starts at the earliest dated BTC/GBP price in its verified loaded file. We don't guess prices for earlier dates or claim that 2012 was Bitcoin's first trading day.

Use your own price file instead

Choose a CSV with the columns Date,BTC_GBP (dates as YYYY-MM-DD, prices in pounds). We never make up prices — rows we can't read are skipped, and journeys with big holes in the data are refused.

Please read: Time Machine is a simulation using past daily closing prices. It never touches real money, exchange accounts or API keys, and it cannot place real trades. Past results are not forecasts and nothing here is financial advice. Real life has costs this simulation leaves out — price differences between buying and selling, delays, minimum order sizes, and tax on any gains. Bitcoin prices from its earliest years are thin and less reliable. Bitcoin can lose most of its value.
