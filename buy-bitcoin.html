🌱 First, a word of caution

Don't invest unless you're prepared to lose all the money you invest. Bitcoin is a high-risk, volatile asset. UK crypto holdings are generally not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme (FSCS). No app or exchange can remove market risk. Bitcoin Garden is educational, not personal investment advice.

And remember: you can buy a fraction of a Bitcoin. You don't need to buy a whole coin. Consider learning with a very small amount first, if you decide to buy at all.

🪙 Six simple steps

  1. Choose a provider. Use its official website or app-store listing — never an unsolicited link or advertisement.
  2. Create and verify your account. In the UK you may need ID checks, a risk questionnaire and a cooling-off period.
  3. Secure it before funding. Use a unique password and enable two-factor authentication (2FA), preferably an authenticator or passkey where supported.
  4. Add a small amount of money. Read the bank-transfer or card funding fees.
  5. Select Bitcoin (BTC). Check that you are buying actual Bitcoin, not a leveraged product, derivative or unrelated token.
  6. Review before buying. Confirm the GBP cost, quoted BTC amount, trading fee, spread and any withdrawal fees. Save the transaction record.

A spread is the difference between the buy and sell prices. It can matter even when a service advertises a low headline fee.

🔐 Keeping your Bitcoin safe

🛡️ 2FA & passwords

Use a strong, unique password in a password manager. Enable 2FA; app-based codes, passkeys or security keys are generally preferable to SMS where offered. Secure your email account too.

🕵️ Avoid scams

Ignore anyone promising guaranteed returns, asking you to “verify” your wallet, or contacting you unexpectedly as “support”. Never share one-time login codes.

🗝️ Keys & wallets

Leaving Bitcoin with a provider involves custody risk. A private wallet gives more control but more responsibility. Never share your seed/recovery phrase; store it offline and never enter it into a random website.

Sending Bitcoin? Check the network and address carefully. Use a small test transfer first if appropriate; transactions are generally irreversible.

🧾 What about tax in the UK?

For most individuals, simply buying Bitcoin and holding it does not itself trigger Capital Gains Tax (CGT). Tax may arise when you dispose of it, including selling for pounds, swapping into another cryptoasset, spending it, or giving it away (with some exceptions). HMRC guidance ↗

£3,000

Annual CGT exempt amount for most UK individuals in 2026–27. This applies to total qualifying gains, not separately to each Bitcoin sale.

18% / 24%

Generally the individual CGT rates on gains made from 6 April 2026, depending on taxable income and the applicable rate bands.

Keep records

Record date, GBP value, amount of BTC and fees for each purchase, sale or exchange. The UK has pooling and 30-day matching rules; calculations may be more complex than sale price minus one purchase price.

Do I owe tax if the price goes up but I don't sell?

For an ordinary personal investment, an unrealised increase in Bitcoin's price generally doesn't create CGT on its own. Receiving Bitcoin as employment income, rewards or through other activities can involve different tax rules.

Do swaps or purchases count?

Yes, swapping Bitcoin for another cryptocurrency or spending it on goods or services can count as a disposal for CGT purposes, even if no pounds arrive in your bank account.

Will the exchange report transactions to HMRC?

UK cryptoasset service providers must collect relevant customer and transaction information under the Cryptoasset Reporting Framework from 2026, with the first reports due in 2027. You remain responsible for your own records and tax obligations.

What if I buy Bitcoin through my company?

Company-owned cryptoassets have different accounting and tax treatment from an individual's holdings. Ask your accountant rather than applying the personal £3,000 allowance or individual CGT rates.
